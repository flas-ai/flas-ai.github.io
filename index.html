Beyond Steering Vectors:
Flow-based Activation Steering for Inference-Time Intervention

Zehao Jin Ruixuan Deng Junran Wang Xinjie Shen Chao Zhang
arXiv2605.05892 Codeflas-ai/FLAS 🤗 Model2B 🤗 Model9B 🤗 Demotry it →

FLAS learns a concept-conditioned velocity field $v_\theta(h, t, c)$ that transports an unsteered LLM activation $h$ into a steered one by integrating a flow ODE. The flow time $T$ is a continuous, zero-shot strength knob. FLAS is the first learned steering method to consistently beat in-context prompting on AxBench.

1 How it works

Conventional activation steering adds a fixed direction $h \mapsto h + \alpha v$. This is a single Euler step from a single offset — it ignores where you are in activation space. FLAS instead learns a velocity field conditioned on the concept embedding $c$ and on a continuous flow time $t \in [0, T]$:

$$h' \;=\; \varphi_T(h) \;=\; h \;+\; \int_0^T v_\theta\!\bigl(\varphi_t(h),\, t,\, c\bigr)\, dt.$$

We integrate this ODE with a few Euler steps at the chosen layer during the LM's normal forward pass. Because we sample $T \sim \mathrm{U}[T_\min, T_\max]$ during training, a single checkpoint exposes a continuous strength knob at inference — no per-strength fine-tuning, no per-concept training.

Concept-conditioned

One frozen base LM, one ConceptEncoder. Any new concept is just a text prompt — no per-concept optimisation.

Continuous strength

Slide $T$ from 0 (no steering) to ~3 (saturated). Trained once, dialed at inference.

Beats prompting

First learned steering method to consistently outperform in-context prompting on AxBench Concept16k held-out splits.
